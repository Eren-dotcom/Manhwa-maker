# 01 · What manhwa actually is

> **One-line definition:** *manhwa* (만화) is Korean comics. A *webtoon* (웹툰) is the vertical-scroll, full-colour digital format that most modern manhwa is published in. When people say "manhwa" online they almost always mean the webtoon format — but the craft rules below are what make it recognisably Korean rather than a manga page rotated 90°.

## The three cousins, and why the difference matters

| | Manhwa (만화) | Manga (漫画) | Manhua (漫画) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | Korea | Japan | China |
| Reading direction | **Left → right** (print and digital) | Right → left | Left → right |
| Typical colour | Full colour (webtoon), B&W (print) | Mostly B&W | Full colour |
| Typical shape | Vertical scroll, no page breaks | Paginated tankōbon / magazine | Vertical scroll or paginated |
| Publication | Platform-first (Naver, Kakao, Lezhin, Tapas, WEBTOON) | Magazine → volume | Platform-first (Bilibili, Kuaikan) |

**Consequence for direction:** in manhwa, reading order inside a panel is left-to-right, top-to-bottom — the same as English. Do not mirror pages. Do not "flip for Western release". The only thing that changes in vertical scroll is that *down* replaces *page-turn*.

## The format that defines the craft

A webtoon episode is one continuous image, typically 690–800px wide and anywhere from 15,000 to 60,000px tall, cut into panels with gutters. There is no spread, no page turn, and no fixed page height. Three things follow, and they are the source of nearly every rule in this studio:

1. **The scroll is the clock.** Time passes as the reader's thumb moves. A tall panel makes time slow down; a short one snaps it forward.
2. **Every screen is a cliff.** The reader can leave at any pixel. You must give them a reason to keep going at the bottom edge of *every scroll unit*.
3. **Phones are the primary surface.** Design at 360–430px of physical width, arm's length, often on a commute. Anything that only reads on a desktop monitor is decoration, not storytelling.

## A short history you should be able to summarise

- **1990s–2000s:** Korean print manhwa matures (action, romance, sports, horror), heavily influenced by Japanese manga but with its own LTR grammar and a strong *jjikgi* (찍기, "stamp") speed-line tradition.
- **2003–2004:** Daum and Naver launch webtoon portals; vertical scroll becomes the default because it suits scrolling web pages. Free, weekly, ad-supported.
- **2010s:** Mobile explodes. KakaoPage introduces wait-to-free monetisation; Lezhin pioneers paid adult/indie webtoons; Naver's *Challenge* system lets anyone publish.
- **2014–present:** Global expansion via WEBTOON (Naver's English service), Tapas, and licensed translations. Korean webtoons become a major IP pipeline for drama and film adaptation.
- **Now:** AI-assisted production is normal in the pipeline (backgrounds, colour flats, 3D reference), while the highest-value work — writing, staging, character acting — is still human-directed. This studio is built around that division of labour.

## Sub-genres that define the current market

| Genre engine | The promise to the reader | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hunter / System / Gate** | Competence fantasy with visible numbers | Stats replace stakes; the protagonist stops needing anyone |
| **Regression / Reincarnation / Isekai** | A second chance, with knowledge as leverage | The reader knows the outcome before the hero does |
| **Office romance / Contract romance** | Proximity + a secret that keeps them apart | The obstacle could be solved by one honest conversation |
| **School / Bullying revenge** | Justice, delivered with the reader's anger | Violence escalates without moral cost |
| **Dark fantasy / Folklore** | A world with rules older than the characters | Worldbuilding replaces character desire |
| **Thriller / Crime** | Dread + competence | The mystery is solved by information the reader never had |
| **Sports** | Mastery, earned frame by frame | Montage replaces practice |

Choose one engine and one *counter*-engine. *Hunter + office comedy* is a pitch. *Hunter + system + regression + romance + horror* is a mood board.

## What "Korean" means on the page

These are the details that make a series feel native rather than translated:

- **Hierarchy is visible.** Age and seniority change posture, eye-level, who speaks first, and whether a character bows at the waist or just nods.
- **Register carries relationships.** A shift from formal (존댓말) to casual (반말) is a plot event, not a style choice. See [doc 09](09-korean-language-and-localization.md).
- **Institutions are characters.** Bureaus, guilds, chaebol offices, schools and hospital wards are drawn as systems with forms, stamps and fluorescent lighting.
- **Domestic interiors are specific.** Ondol floors, shoe racks at the door, kimchi fridges, convenience-store glow, apartment corridors with one flickering tube light.
- **Food is emotional.** Ramen at 2am, a shared tangerine, a lunch box left on a desk. Meals are how Korean comics show care.
- **Silence is allowed to be long.** A held panel of rain is not a failure of pacing; it is the format's greatest luxury.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **회차 (hoecha)** | Episode / chapter. Weekly cadence is the industry default. |
| **연출 (yeonchul)** | Direction — camera, staging, panel flow. A named job. |
| **콘티 (konti)** | Storyboard. Done before line art, reviewed by the editor. |
| **작화 (jakhwa)** | The drawing/art production itself. |
| **채색 (chaesaek)** | Colouring. |
| **식자 (sikja)** | Lettering. |
| **세로 스크롤** | Vertical scroll. |
| **개미지옥** | "Ant hell" — the trap of a scroll with no reason to stop; used as a warning in editorial. |
| **떡밥** | Bait — a plot hook deliberately left unresolved to keep readers subscribed. |

## Non-negotiables to carry into every stage

1. Design for a phone, then check on a desktop — never the reverse.
2. Colour is the norm, and colour is a *narrative* tool: palette shifts mark location, time and emotional state.
3. Reading order is left-to-right, and every lettering decision must respect it.
4. Weekly cadence means the episode is a product, not a portfolio piece. Ship a clean 45-panel episode rather than a beautiful 12-panel fragment.
5. The last panel of the episode is the most valuable real estate you own.

# 10 · Publishing, platform & business

> The craft gets you read. The business decides whether you keep making it.

## What a platform is buying

1. **Retention**, not quality. The percentage of readers who finish episode 1 and open episode 2.
2. **A repeatable engine.** A series that can produce 40 good episodes, not one great one.
3. **Genre clarity.** The editor must be able to describe it in one line to a marketing team.
4. **Reliability.** Weekly delivery, clean files, no surprises.
5. **A signature image.** Something that works as a thumbnail and a poster.

## The first three episodes

| Episode | Job |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | Prove the promise. Hook in 3 panels, one full demonstration of the engine, hard cliffhanger. |
| **2** | Widen the world by one room and one character. Same engine, bigger stakes. |
| **3** | First real reversal — the protagonist loses something they wanted. This is where retention is won or lost. |

Most platforms will judge a series on a 3-episode sample. Write those three as a unit before you write episode 4.

## Monetisation models you should understand

- **Wait-to-free**: readers wait N hours per episode or pay to unlock early. Revenue scales with impatience, which scales with cliffhanger quality.
- **Fast Pass / paid early**: a fixed window of paid access before free release.
- **Coins / cookies**: per-episode purchase; common on mature and thriller platforms.
- **Ad-supported free**: revenue from platform ads; the creator is paid by views/contract.
- **IP licensing**: drama, film, game, goods. For a hit series this dwarfs page revenue, and it is won by *premise* and *character*, not panel count.
- **Direct support**: Patreon, Ko-fi, physical zines, commissions. Best for niche or mature work.

## Contracts: the four clauses to read first

1. **Rights granted** — translation, print, adaptation, merchandising. Perpetual, worldwide grants are common; understand what you are selling.
2. **Exclusivity** — can you publish the same series elsewhere or in print?
3. **Revenue share and reporting** — percentage, recoupment, payment schedule, statements.
4. **Termination / reversion** — how do you get the rights back if the platform drops the series?

Get a lawyer who reads entertainment contracts in your jurisdiction. This document is not legal advice.

## Marketing that actually moves a webtoon

- **The signature panel.** One image per episode that is designed to be shared. Put it in units 6–8 where the emotion peaks.
- **Thumbnail discipline.** Face, emotion, one prop, high contrast, readable at 120px. Test three variants.
- **First-three-panels test.** Strip the captions and balloons from panels 1–3. If the promise is not visible, redesign them.
- **Consistency over virality.** Weekly delivery builds a habit; a viral panel builds a spike. You need both, in that order.
- **Community:** reply to comments in the first hour after release. Platforms weight engagement in recommendations.
- **Cross-platform**: publish a vertical-friendly cut on socials, but link to the platform that pays you.

## Metrics to watch (and what they mean)

| Metric | Meaning | Healthy direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Episode 1 → 2 retention | Did the hook work? | Up; below ~50% is a hook problem |
| Completion rate per episode | Did the pacing work? | Up; drop-off clusters show flat units |
| Subscription conversion | Did the ending work? | Up; cliffhanger quality |
| Comments per 1k views | Did it make people feel something? | Up |
| Unlock conversion (paid) | Did impatience work? | Up on paid platforms |

Find *where* readers leave. If they leave at unit 4, you have a turn problem. If they leave at the last screen, you have a cliffhanger problem.

## Budgeting a season

- **Writing + storyboard**: 1–2 weeks per episode for a strong solo creator.
- **Art**: 6–10 finished panels per day with AI assistance and a reference library.
- **Lettering**: half a day per episode once the studio is set up.
- **Buffer**: keep 3 episodes ahead before launch. Weekly serialisation without a buffer is how series die in episode 9.
- **Cost**: per-episode generation + cleanup + tools. Track it per panel so you can price a contract.

## Pitching

A one-page pitch contains: title, logline, hook image, genre + engine, comparables, 3-episode synopsis, 8-panel sample sequence, style reference (your own art or a clearly labelled AI-reference treatment), cadence, and your production capacity.

The sample sequence matters more than the synopsis. Nobody believes a synopsis.

## The long game

The webtoon market rewards series that *keep* their promise for 100+ episodes. The way to survive that is:

1. A premise with a built-in engine (something that generates a new problem every week).
2. A bible detailed enough that episode 60 still sounds like episode 1.
3. A production pipeline with buffers and reusable assets.
4. A signature visual identity that is cheap for you to reproduce and impossible to confuse with anyone else.

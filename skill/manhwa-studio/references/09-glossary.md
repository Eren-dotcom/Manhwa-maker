# 09 — Glossary

**Arc** — a run of 8–12 episodes with its own dramatic question and turning points.

**Beat** — the smallest narrative unit a panel delivers. The toolkit's `beat` field also selects a
lighting fragment (see `02-panel-grammar.md`).

**Bleed** — a panel that runs the full platform width, ignoring the side safe-margins. Used for
establishing shots and climaxes.

**Canvas (WEBTOON Canvas)** — the self-publishing tier of WEBTOON. No approval, no exclusivity, you
keep the IP, ad revenue share after thresholds (~1,000 subscribers + ~40,000 monthly views).

**CFG (classifier-free guidance)** — how hard the model obeys the prompt. 5–7 for SDXL anime; too
high burns the colours, too low ignores you.

**Cliffhanger** — the final 2–3 panels that make the next episode necessary. In vertical scroll, the
strongest image of the sequence belongs at the bottom of the screen so the reader must scroll to it.

**ComfyUI** — node-graph Stable Diffusion frontend; the standard for headless, repeatable, batchable
generation. Workflows export as JSON (API format) and can be driven by scripts.

**ControlNet** — conditioning that forces structure (pose, depth, lineart) from a reference image. Use
it for *composition*, not for identity.

**Cut** — Korean industry term for a panel/frame. "68 cuts per episode" is the industry's standing
workload figure.

**Fast Pass / wait-or-pay** — platform model where the newest 3–4 episodes are paid or waited for;
a reason escalating cliffhangers are structurally baked into the form.

**Gutter** — the vertical gap between panels. Carries *time*: 80px is fast, 200px is normal, 600px+
is a beat landing or a scene change.

**Headroom** — drawing at 1.5–2.5× the publish width so downscaled line art stays sharp and print
stays possible.

**Hook** — the image or line in panels 1–5 that decides whether a stranger keeps scrolling. You get
about eight seconds.

**Hires-fix** — a second, short diffusion pass after a latent upscale. Sharpens faces, eyes and line
art more than extra sampling steps do.

**Illustrious / NoobAI / Animagine / Pony** — the main SDXL-family anime checkpoints. Illustrious for
the LoRA ecosystem, Animagine for a permissive licence, NoobAI for booru coverage (non-commercial).

**IP-Adapter** — conditions a generation on a reference image. FaceID / Plus variants transfer
identity; weaker on anime style than a trained LoRA.

**LoRA** — a small adapter trained on 10–40 images that teaches a base model a character, an outfit or
a style. The only reliable long-series consistency method.

**Manhwa** — Korean comics. Print-era term; today mostly synonymous with the vertical-scroll webtoon
form.

**Midpoint hook** — the second hook placed at 30–40% through an episode, right where the largest
reader drop-off happens.

**Originals** — WEBTOON's curated, contracted, exclusive tier: an editor, an advance, a schedule.
Entry is by invitation or contest, usually after months of Canvas.

**Panel sheet** — this toolkit's script format: one JSON object per panel with shot, beat, pace,
cast, action and dialogue.

**Placeholder** — a composited stand-in panel drawn when art is missing. Lets you check layout and
pacing before generating anything. Never ship one.

**Prompt block** — the frozen, verbatim character description reused in every prompt.

**Safe margin** — the outer ~40px at 800px width. Readers skim the edges; keep faces and text inside
the middle 80%.

**Seed** — the random number that makes a generation reproducible. Fixed per character, varied per
panel, gives you controlled iteration instead of gambling.

**SFX** — sound effect lettering. Typography, never a speech bubble, never generated by a model.

**Tile** — one uploaded image of the sliced strip. WEBTOON Canvas: 800px wide, ≤1280px tall, ≤2MB, up
to 100 per episode.

**Vertical scroll** — the webtoon reading format: one continuous strip read top to bottom on a phone,
no pages, no spreads. The unit is the episode.
